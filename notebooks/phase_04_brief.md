## Business Model

Symbotic's business model involves developing, commercializing, and deploying innovative and comprehensive technology solutions that dramatically improve supply chain operations [Q1-D1]. They automate the processing of pallets, cases, and items in warehouses using robotic-based automation systems, which include hardware and essential software [Q1-D1]. Their systems are operational in a number of the world’s largest retailers, including Walmart, and wholesale distributors [Q1-D1].

## Customers

Symbotic's main customers include Walmart, Albertsons, C&S Wholesale Grocers, GreenBox, and Target [Q2-D1].

## Risks

The main risks associated with Symbotic's business include extreme volatility in the stock market [Q3-D1]. Potential challenges with the implementation of their enterprise resource planning system may impact their business and operations [Q3-D2]. Symbotic may not achieve or maintain profitability in the near term or at all [Q3-D2].

## Recent News

Symbotic will introduce advanced Nyobolt battery technology for its SymBot autonomous mobile robots in September 2025 [Q4-F1]. The SEC has closed its investigation into Symbotic Inc. regarding potential Rule 21F-17 violations and the company's 2024 revenue restatement [Q4-F2]. Symbotic's most recent acquisition is ARMS, a Provider of automation services, acquired in July 2026 [Q4-F3].

## Competitors

Swisslog, Greyorange, Autostore, and Element Logic are the largest Symbotic competitors [Q5-F1]. Vecna Robotics, Locus Robotics, and Mytra are top competitors of Symbotic [Q5-F2]. Amazon Robotics, CyPhy Works, and iRobot are competitors of Symbotic LLC [Q5-F3].

## Publicly Reported Risks

A significant portion of Symbotic's revenues is derived from its partnership with Walmart, which serves as the largest customer [Q6-F1]. More than 84% of Symbotic's total revenues were generated from Walmart, which poses significant risks to the company's revenue growth and stability [Q6-F2]. Symbotic’s warehouse automation systems have primarily been installed at Walmart distribution centers, which have accounted for ~50-80% of the company’s revenue [Q6-F3]. The main risks associated with Symbotic's business include challenges with the implementation of a new enterprise resource planning system, potential conflicts of interest due to shared executives with a key customer, and substantial payments required to equity holders of New Symbotic Holdings for certain tax benefits [Q6-D1]. Additionally, disruptions to the supply chain, failure to adapt to rapidly changing technology, and stagnation or decline in demand for their systems are significant risks [Q6-D2].
